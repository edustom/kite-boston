# KiteBoston 2026 — Claude Context

## Overview

This project is a minimalist, content-first website for a one-person kiteboarding instructor based in Nahant, Massachusetts (KiteBoston).

This is NOT a typical sports school website.
It represents a low-volume, experience-driven offering focused on getting students to true independence as efficiently as possible.

---

## Tone & Voice (CRITICAL)

### Core Characteristics

- Direct, calm, experienced
- Not salesy, not corporate
- No hype language
- No exaggerated claims
- No generic marketing phrasing

If any sentence sounds like a generic watersports or surf school website, it must be rewritten.

---

### Style Guidelines

- Write like a single experienced person, not a company
- Understated expertise (no bragging, no over-explaining)
- Clear and concise language
- Slightly selective tone (not for everyone, but not elitist)
- Respect the reader’s intelligence (no coddling or oversimplifying)

---

### What to Avoid (STRICT)

- “We are passionate about…”
- “Our mission is…”
- “Experience the thrill…”
- “Premier kiteboarding experience…”
- Any corporate or template-style phrasing

Avoid:
- Urgency tactics
- Aggressive CTAs
- Overly friendly or exaggerated tone

---

## Positioning

KiteBoston is:

- A one-person instruction offering
- Focused on real progression and independence
- Based on real wind conditions, not fixed schedules
- Designed for people serious about learning

It is NOT:

- A volume-driven school
- A beginner “experience” provider
- A heavily branded or commercial operation

---

## Key Messaging Pillars

### Independence
The goal is for the student to not need the instructor anymore.

### Efficiency
Time is spent on real progression, not wasted hours.

### Real Conditions
Lessons are scheduled based on wind and conditions.

### Guidance
Students receive help avoiding beginner mistakes, especially with gear.

---

## Strategic Friction (IMPORTANT)

The site should gently filter out poor-fit users.

Examples of acceptable tone:
- “I take on a small number of dedicated students.”
- “If you're just looking to try it once, there are better options elsewhere.”

This is intentional and should be preserved.

---

## Design Principles

- Minimalist, mobile-first
- Content-first (design must not dominate content)
- Subtle personality through typography and spacing
- No heavy UI elements (no cards, shadows, gradients)
- Avoid startup/agency aesthetic
- Slightly editorial layout (not perfectly centered)

---

## Technical Requirements

- Plain HTML + CSS only (no frameworks)
- Fast loading, lightweight
- Use Google Fonts:
- Inter (body)
- Instrument Serif (headings)
- Semantic HTML
- Clean, readable structure
- Include image placeholders:
- `/images/hero.jpg`
- `/images/lesson.jpg`
- etc.

### Section: Lessons ([ #lessons ])

**Purpose:** Explain how it works

**Structure:**
- Expected progression (3–5 sessions)
- Pricing philosophy:
- Hourly rate
- Only charged for real progression time
- How lessons work:
- Wind-based scheduling
- Water-first approach
- Progression path:
- Lessons → Ride Along → Independence
- Gear philosophy:
- Do NOT encourage early purchase
- Instructor helps guide decisions
- Fit section:
- Who it’s for
- Subtle mention of group learning
- Light mention of under 18
- Soft CTA → apply

---

### Section: Apply ([ #apply ])

**Purpose:** Filter and convert

**Structure:**
- Intro (encourage reading lessons page first)
- Pre-frame (who this works best for)
- Form section (Tally embed placeholder)
- Expectation setting:
- Not all applicants scheduled immediately
- Wind-dependent scheduling

## Form Structure (for reference)

Fields to include:
- Name
- Email
- Phone
- Experience level
- Goals
- Availability
- Weight (for kite sizing)
- Comfortable in water (yes/no)
- Optional: learning with a friend

Form should be:
- Simple
- Clean
- Not overwhelming

## Content Rules

- Keep writing tight and intentional
- Avoid long blocks of text
- Break content into natural sections
- Do NOT default to FAQ-heavy structure unless necessary
- Prioritize clarity over completeness

---

## UX / Interaction

- Soft CTAs (no aggressive buttons)
- No “conversion tactics”
- Navigation simple and minimal
- Mobile-first spacing and readability

Minimal, understated, experience-driven kite instruction site that feels personal, selective, and practical — never salesy, never corporate.
